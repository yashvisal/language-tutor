# Audit: where the app stands and what shipping needs (2026-08-23)

*Written by Fable at Yash's request while he was away. Sources: the live
sessions of 2026-08-21/22/23 and their logs, PR #5 and its review, and three
read-only code audits run this evening (signed-in UI, tutor arc, gap to
shipping). Nothing here is a decision; the decisions are yours. Read
`product-vision.md` first if anything below seems to contradict it — the
vision wins.*

## 1. What changed today, and what it proved

Live testing found five things, all fixed on `phase-5-product-shell`:

| Felt | Cause (verified in logs/code) | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Talking over the tutor left it "speaking from history" | LiveKit's adaptive interruption detector ruled a real interjection "false" 3× and resumed the tutor over the learner | Interruption mode → plain VAD. Learner speech > ~0.5 s stops the tutor, deterministically. Recorded in the vision doc. |
| Long silence before the first replies | A hedged sentence scored 0.24 end-of-turn confidence → detector waited the full 6 s max | Max endpointing 6 s → 3 s |
| Words after a pause landing in the previous bubble | Hold flush waited 1.0 s for the STT final; STT needs ~1.2 s → provider never closed its segment, replayed pre-hold words after resume | Flush wait → 2.5 s (hold is acked first, so no felt cost) |
| Next sentence appended to the previous bubble (the long-standing "prototypey" feel) | The UI closed a learner bubble only when the *analyzer* answered — ~2 s after the worker had committed the turn. Speak inside that window and you joined the old bubble. | **The worker now publishes `tutor.turn_seq` on every learner-turn commit; the UI closes the bubble on that.** Fragments within a turn still join. (`93cd520`) |
| Onboarding flicker (`/home` before `/welcome`) | Redirect decided client-side, then Clerk's fixed post-sign-in URL | Server-side gate + a `/go` decision route; the browser never touches a route it isn't staying on |

Also: light-mode retune of the landing (washes and panel tints where dark had
glow), the merged single landing page, and the Clerk `aud`/`email` JWT claims.

**Verified live:** sign-up → grant → `/home`, session start, Ask answering,
Review generating, resume-after-Ask picking up the question, VAD cut-off.
**Not yet verified live:** the turn-commit bubble fix (needs a session) and a
deliberate mid-word hold (`hold: … flushed result=committed` should show).

## 2. The tutor arc — assessment

*Resolved 2026-08-24: Yash replaced the fixed 10-minute session with a metered
conversation and dropped the arc entirely — see the vision doc and
`phases/phase-6-metered-conversation.md`. The analysis below stands as the
reasoning; Option A's "support on evidence" survives as the tutor's behaviour.*

*(From the arc audit; read the full reasoning in the agent report if wanted.
This is the part that answers "rethink the 10-minute session vs 5-minute
chunks".)*

**What a learner actually gets in the first 90 seconds:** the greeting asks
"are you ready?" in English; the frame block asks for one try; the guided
block, arriving silently at 60 s, asks "up for some bits?"; the scene block
(never reached in testing) asks "ready to do it for real?". **Four consent
gates**, all in English, on a product whose success line is "I speak Spanish…
then I keep talking." That is the mechanical feel. Free conversation gets a
scenario invented for it because `"free conversation"` is a scenario string,
not a null plan. Debrief and the clock's wrap-up both fire at 9:00 and
contradict each other ("do not say goodbye" vs "bring it to a close").

**Root cause, structural:** phases ride *elapsed seconds*. The arc can't see
whether the learner produced a sentence, is stuck, or said yes — so consent
had to be written into every prompt as a question. The gates are a symptom of
a blind state machine, not a pedagogy choice. And with 1/4/4/1 against 10
minutes, 80% of the arc is unreachable at the 2–5 minute sessions people
actually run.

**Option A — conversation-driven arc, same 10 minutes.** Feed the arc what the
worker already knows and discards: committed turns, correction density,
anchor-language ratio, hold/Ask events. `OPEN → FLOW ⇄ SUPPORT → (DEEPEN) →
CLOSE`. Open in Spanish with one easy question (the pre-flight *was* the
consent). Free conversation opens with "what do you want to talk about?" and
the answer becomes the subject. Guided stops being a phase and becomes a
*repair mode* entered on evidence (empty turn, 3+ errors, mostly English, an
Ask) and exited after one exchange. The scene is offered inside the
conversation, once. Only CLOSE stays on the clock, because the clock is money.
Every existing seam (instruction updates, hold deferral, clock) survives.

**Option B — two 5-minute chunks.** A chunk is a full mini-arc; at 5:00 the
existing pause overlay opens as a break (Review regenerated from the
transcript, Ask a tap away), unmetered by construction; continue starts chunk
two *from* the break. Billing unchanged (actual minutes, rounded up). Cost
lower. Risk: the break is an exit ramp at exactly the moment learners are
warming up — sessions already end at 2–5 minutes.

**Recommendation:** A, and take one thing from B — the mid-session breath as an
*offer* the tutor makes when the learner has been working hard, not a wall at
5:00. Chunking ratifies the symptom (short sessions) and doubles the opening
logistics that are the actual complaint.

**Five changes worth making regardless:**
1. Delete three of the four consent gates (`prompts.py:134`, `:219`, `:231`);
   keep none in the greeting.
2. Open in the target language with one short question.
3. Make `free conversation` a null plan that asks first (backlog #1).
4. Reframe guided from "the pattern, repeated" to repair-on-evidence.
5. Separate debrief from wrap-up — or drop the spoken debrief and let the
   post-session summary carry it (vision already puts deep coaching after
   the conversation).

Related, already in the backlog: Review should follow the conversation, not
the plan (#2) — it fits Option A naturally (regenerate on hold from the
transcript-so-far).

## 3. The signed-in UI — assessment

*(From the UI audit, verified with file:line. Verdict in one line: the
landing established a design grammar — `max-w-6xl` sections, hairlines at
`border/60`, one eyebrow style, blue-500 as the single accent, text at full
opacity, reduced-motion respected — and almost none of it survived into the
product, which is a second, quieter system tuned on a dark stage.)*

Ranked, most user-visible first:

1. **`/session` is a cul-de-sac.** Pre-flight and summary render outside the
   shell with no header, no wordmark, no way to `/home` except browser-back
   (`session-summary.tsx:105-116`). M.
2. **A debug readout ships on the live stage** — `held · history+correction`
   in mono, bottom-right, always (`conversation-stage.tsx:426-429`). S.
3. **`/home` is a balance line bolted onto the pre-flight.** One balance state
   (the plan's fine/low/empty is unimplemented), Start enabled at 0 minutes,
   token route still hard-codes `max_minutes` and `user_id: null`, no sessions
   list, and `return null` while Convex loads so the shell flashes an empty
   body. L — because the metering seam is genuinely missing (see §4).
4. **Four eyebrow/label styles** for one idea (landing, `Overline`,
   `stage-grid`/`study-review`, inline copies in summary and correction mark).
   S.
5. **Two accent colors.** Landing hardcodes `blue-500/600`; the product uses
   `--primary` (a deeper indigo); the sidebar has a third. M — point
   `--primary` at the Aura blue, replace the landing's literals with the
   token.
6. **The light-mode problem, precisely:** product text is routinely
   `muted-foreground` at 50–70% opacity (`overline.tsx:21`,
   `session-preflight.tsx:248,278`, `study-*.tsx`, `minutes-pill.tsx:41`) —
   ~#c4c4c4 on white, under 3:1 contrast. Hairlines at `border/40`. Reads fine
   on dark, which is why it shipped. M — ban sub-`/70` text opacity; add a
   per-theme subtle-foreground token.
7. **`/settings` looks like a stub**: no top padding under the header,
   gutter misaligned with the shell, two fields, then whitespace. M.
8. **Shell header is empty chrome**; sidebar says `Tutor` while the landing
   says `tutor`; no theme toggle inside the app (only the hidden `d` hotkey,
   which also fires mid-session). S.
9. **Motion**: the product never checks reduced-motion; the held "breathing
   ring" re-explains a state already shown three ways. S.
10. **Duplicates**: pre-flight `Chip` ≡ `LevelPicker` inline (only one has the
    ARIA radio pattern); correction diff row written twice; level asked in
    three places with three shapes. S.
11. **Study surface**: Ask is a raw textarea with no send button (Enter only —
    undiscoverable, no pointer path); three empty states in three voices, one
    of them "the analyzer". M.
12. **Dead affordances**: disabled "Buy more minutes" with a TODO above it;
    "Suggest one for me" at heading altitude; vocab input commits on blur
    silently. S.
13. `/welcome` floats on white with no wordmark; heading a full scale below
    the landing's. S.
14. `/terms`, `/privacy`: a fifth heading scale, container narrower than the
    chrome. S.
15. Leftovers: `min-h-screen` on the sign-in fallback; `/design-inspo` public
    with a raw file path in copy; pre-flight promises "10 minutes" from a
    constant, not the balance.

**If we only fix five:** (1) delete the debug readout and the dead buy
button; (2) give `/session` a way home; (3) kill sub-`/70` text opacity and
give light mode a real subtle token; (4) one accent, one eyebrow, one chip;
(5) finish `/home`'s three balance states and stop the blank-shell flash.

## 5. Proposed order of work

The honest picture: **the loop is close to right and the product around it is
a scaffold.** Nothing below is a surprise — it is phase 5's remaining build
order plus the quality debt the audits made specific. The sequencing question
is whether to fix *feel* or *money* first. My recommendation: the cheap feel
fixes now, the arc next, then the money seam — because the arc decides what a
session *is*, and the money seam meters it; metering the wrong thing well is
wasted work. Stripe and deploy come last because they are the least uncertain.

**0. Verify today (next session).** Turn-commit bubbles, a deliberate mid-word
hold, VAD cut-off, 3 s endpointing. Then merge PR #5.

**1. Quick quality (S items, one Opus batch, about a day).** The UI audit's
"five things": delete the debug readout and the dead buy button; give
`/session` a way home; ban sub-`/70` text opacity with a per-theme subtle
token (this *is* the light-mode fix); one accent (`--primary` = the Aura
blue), one `Overline`, one chip; stop the blank-shell flash. Plus wordmark
casing, a theme toggle in the shell, reduced-motion in the session components,
`min-h-svh` on sign-in.

**2. The arc, Option A (design first, then build).** Decision needed: A
(conversation-driven, recommended) vs B (5-minute chunks). Either way the five
"regardless" changes go in first: no consent gates, open in Spanish, free
conversation asks, guided = repair-on-evidence, debrief moves to the summary.
Then feed the arc turn evidence — `turn_seq` already exists; the signal that
fixed the bubbles is the arc's first input. Review regenerates from the
transcript on hold (backlog #2). This is the work that makes it "cute".

**3. The money seam (phase-5 step 2, the largest gap).** Token gating,
`sessions` rows, the Convex HTTP debit action, balance states, the
agent-disconnect state, a token-route rate limit, the trial-model decision.
Exit check the plan already names: ledger sum == the worker's billed line.

**4. Ship (phase-5 steps 3–4).** Stripe test→live; Clerk prod instance; Convex
prod; Vercel + `lk agent create`; reviewed legal copy; minimal Sentry. Then
the phase-4 exit criterion, run by a stranger.

**Decisions needed:** (a) arc A or B; (b) trial sessions on the mini model or
the full one; (c) pack prices confirmed ($3.99 / $15.99 / $34.99); (d) a
sub-10-minute balance gives a shorter session or blocks (recommend shorter);
(e) step 1 before or after the arc work (recommend before — it is a day and it
changes how every test feels).
